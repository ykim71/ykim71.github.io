projects

Research on political affect across language, images, platforms, and AI.

Selected research

Across these projects, I ask how political information acquires an emotional register, who or what amplifies it, and how those signals travel through public communication.

01

Visual politics · Longitudinal and ongoing

Partisan memes and affective engagement across election cycles

How do partisan memes and the engagement they attract change across election cycles?

This research began with a 2026 Journal of Information Technology & Politics paper examining the composition and amplification of partisan memes across four U.S. election cycles. It has since developed into a broader research program tracing how partisan visual communication and affective engagement evolve over time.

Material
Partisan memes across multiple election cycles
Approach
Computational and human-centered analysis
Focus
Longitudinal patterns in political communication and engagement
View the OSF preregistration
02

Networks · Cross-partisan communication

Cross-ideological sharing and counterargument

When people share content from across the political aisle, are they endorsing it—or arguing against it?

This project studies the relationship between user–elite following networks and the language people use when political information crosses ideological boundaries.

Material
User–elite interactions on Twitter/X
Approach
Bayesian network modeling
Signal
Counterargument detection
Explore the Harvard Dataverse
03

Political language · Longitudinal

Affective language and polarization in the U.S. public

Does the emotional register used by partisan actors move with affective polarization in the public?

A large-scale study of partisan Facebook pages connects changes in political language to changes in affective polarization over more than a decade.

Material
≈14 million Facebook posts
Period
2008–2020
Approach
NLP + time-series modeling
